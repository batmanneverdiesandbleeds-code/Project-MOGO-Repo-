BIOLOGICAL ANOMALY: FLUID REDISTRIBUTION

1G (EARTH GRAVITY) 0G (MICROGRAVITY)
On Earth, gravity pulls bodily fluids downward into the legs. The cardiovascular system works constantly to pump it back up.
"Puffy Head, Bird Legs" Syndrome In microgravity, fluids shift upward into the torso and head. This causes facial swelling, nasal congestion, and a significant thinning of the legs as fluid volume leaves the lower extremities.

MISSION BRIEFINGS (FAQ)

Astronauts use the tops of their feet to hook into handrails for stability while floating. Result: baby-soft soles (since they never walk), and heavily calloused tops of their feet.
A vestibular mismatch between the inner ear (feeling free-fall) and the eyes (seeing a stationary cabin) causes severe motion sickness in roughly 50% of crew members during the first 72 hours of flight.
Without gravity to drive natural convection currents (hot air rising), exhaled carbon dioxide forms a static bubble around an astronaut's head. They must sleep near active ventilation fans to avoid suffocation.
Due to the rigid, highly pressurized gloves worn during Extravehicular Activity (EVA), blood flow to the fingertips is restricted. Combined with repetitive impacts against the glove tips, many astronauts lose their fingernails entirely—a condition known as fingernail delamination.
When trying to sleep, astronauts often report seeing bright flashes of light even with their eyes closed. These are caused by high-energy cosmic rays streaking through the spacecraft hull and physically striking the optic nerve or retina.
Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) affects roughly 70% of astronauts on long-duration missions. The fluid shift that causes "Puffy Head" also increases pressure inside the skull, flattening the back of the eyeballs and causing permanent near-sightedness.
Without gravity constantly stressing the skeleton, astronauts can lose up to 1-2% of their bone mass every month. To counter this, they must exercise 2 hours every single day using specialized resistance machines (like ARED).
When astronauts return from spacewalks, the airlock fills with a distinct, pungent odor. Described as smelling like seared steak, hot metal, or gunpowder, this smell is caused by high-energy atomic oxygen clinging to their suits.
